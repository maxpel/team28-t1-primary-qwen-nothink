## 0 · Approach identity and output
- **0.1 Team ★**: RAG team silicon sample study. Members/creators: Farah Adeeba (University of Konstanz); Jing Ma (University of Konstanz); Marcia Ferreira Goncalves (Graphwise); Max Pellert (Barcelona Supercomputing Center). Corresponding contact: max.pellert@bsc.es.
- **0.2 Plain-language summary ★**: We simulate individual survey respondents by prompting a large language model to complete the target study's full survey instrument in character as a demographically specified persona. Each persona is drawn from the U.S. General Social Survey and assigned to one of the 17 conditions (16 interventions + control). For each persona×condition pairing, we generate 10 independent completions and average them into a single submitted response, covering all 13 preregistered outcomes for every persona.
- **0.3 Submission tier & approach family ★**: Tier 1. Approach family: per-respondent simulation, single model, zero-shot (no retrieval, no literature conditioning, no fine-tuning).
- **0.4 Pipeline diagram**:
1. Pool GSS survey waves (2018, 2021, 2022, 2024) into a weighted persona source (within-year weight normalization for equal per-wave sampling probability).
2. Cross-quota match personas to the target study's demographic quota structure (age×gender and gender×race crosses), with an education-skew correction derived from Voelkel et al.'s (2026) published achieved-vs-target recruitment gap. A fresh, independent persona draw is made per condition (not reused across conditions), matching the human study's between-subjects design.
3. Verbalize each persona as a compact 6-field JSON object (gender, age_band, race, education, income, party) embedded in a system prompt instructing the model to fully inhabit the persona rather than respond as an AI assistant.
4. For each persona×condition×repeat: present the intervention stimulus verbatim (or one of three control filler texts, drawn at random, for the control condition), then the full 43-item survey battery as a single structured-JSON-schema request; for this target study specifically, the newsletter-signup item is administered as a genuine second conversational turn, continuing the same conversation with the model's own prior reply included as context.
5. Parse each response against the exact expected JSON key set; responses that fail to parse or are missing expected keys are excluded and logged, not silently retained.
6. Repeat steps 3–5 ten times per persona×condition; average the 10 completions arithmetically into one submitted row per persona (no rounding or thresholding).
7. Compute derived/composite outcome variables per the benchmark's codebook definitions (e.g. funding_perceptions = 100 − funding_5; trust_multidimensional as the mean of its four three-item subscales).
8. Rename/reformat to the Tier 1 submission schema.
- **0.5 Coverage ★**: every submission predicts **all 16 interventions and all 13 outcomes** (partial coverage is not accepted). Confirm here: All 16 interventions and all 13 outcomes covered. 9,912 total respondents, 551 per intervention condition, 1,096 in control. Confirmed.

## A · Scope of LLM use
- **A.1 Purpose**: The LLM is used at exactly one pipeline stage: generating each simulated persona's full survey response (all 13 outcomes, plus the newsletter-signup item as a second turn) per persona×condition×repeat. It is not used for persona construction (GSS-sourced), stimulus selection, or any post-processing/analysis step.
- **A.2 Degree of automation ★**: Fully automated; no human in the loop at generation time. No exceptions.

## B · Model / system details (once per model)
- **B.1 Model name(s)**: Qwen3.6-27B (Alibaba Qwen team), 27B-parameter dense hybrid Gated DeltaNet/attention architecture, released April 22, 2026, Apache 2.0 license. Source: https://huggingface.co/Qwen/Qwen3.6-27B
- **B.2 Access & context mode**: Local inference, self-hosted via vLLM's OpenAI-compatible chat completions API. Stateful, multi-turn conversation per persona (system + stimulus + battery, plus a second user turn for the newsletter item). Generation calls executed: Qwen3.6-27B (thinking disabled), 2026-08-22 23:26:12 to 2026-08-23 07:32:50.
- **B.3 Configuration**: Temperature 0.9, top-p 0.95, thinking mode explicitly disabled (enable_thinking: false), max context length 6,144 tokens, 10 completions per persona×condition. No fixed per-generation seed (vLLM's own internal sampling RNG); reproducibility under identical settings is therefore distributional, not bit-for-bit exact. Reasoning effort: N/A (thinking disabled).
- **B.4 Customization**: None beyond serving-level optimizations: vLLM's automatic prefix caching (--enable-prefix-caching --mamba-cache-mode align), which affects generation speed only, not model outputs. No fine-tuning, no RAG, no tool use, no web search, no agentic scaffolding for this entry.
- **B.5 Persistent memory**: None across different persona×condition×repeat calls; each is an independent conversation. Within a single persona's own two-turn exchange (main battery, then the newsletter offer), the model's own prior reply is included as conversational context, but this does not persist to any other call.
- **B.6 Inference stack**: vLLM 0.27.1, bf16 (no quantization), served on 1 node of MareNostrum 5's accelerated partition (4× NVIDIA H100 64GB), tensor-parallel size 4.
- **B.7 Ensembles**: N/A for this entry (single model). A pooled ensemble of this model with Llama 3.3 70B is submitted as a separate secondary entry with its own registration form.

## C · Prompts
- **C.1 Exact prompts**: Verbatim text deposited alongside the code (see K.1); constants SYSTEM_PROMPT_TEMPLATE, TARGET_BATTERY_PREAMBLE, TARGET_BATTERY_BLOCKS, NEWSLETTER_OFFER_PROMPT in the pipeline source, rendered per-call via render_survey_battery(). Iteratively refined during development against the Voelkel et al. pilot testbed, then held fixed for the actual target-study generation runs reported here.
- **C.2 System-wide instructions**: "You are simulating a single survey respondent, not an AI assistant. Fully inhabit the persona below and answer every item as that person plausibly would — including if their honest answer is inconsistent, apathetic, or skeptical. Do not moderate, hedge, or soften responses toward a 'balanced' or 'safe' answer. Real respondents show a full range of views, including strongly held ones you may not personally agree with. There are no correct answers." (persona JSON follows, then task framing for the survey battery).
- **C.3 Prompt-design rationale**: The "fully inhabit the persona / don't hedge toward a safe answer" instruction was specifically designed to counter a known failure mode in LLM-simulated survey respondents: alignment-driven flattening of response diversity (under-dispersion relative to real human variance). An explicit "respond with ONLY these keys, no additional keys" instruction was added after early testing showed the model occasionally emitting extra unrequested fields (e.g. a stray summary field), which was resolved by this prompt change combined with strict schema validation (see G.2). Structured JSON-schema output was chosen over free text specifically for parsing reliability at scale.

## D · Persona / profile construction (Tiers 1–2)
- **D.1 Profile source**: U.S. General Social Survey (GSS), pooled waves 2018, 2021, 2022, 2024, downloaded via the gssr R package and decoded to real category labels via haven::as_factor() (not raw numeric codes). All six moderator fields (gender, age_band, race, education, income, party) were mapped from GSS's own response categories to the benchmark's exact required category strings, verified directly against the real extracted data rather than assumed from documentation.
- **D.2 Profile verbalization**: Template-based: the six fields are rendered as a compact JSON object embedded directly in the system prompt (not a generated free-text narrative).
- **D.3 Assignment & weighting**: Personas cross-quota matched to the target study's Table 3 (age×gender and gender×race crosses), independence assumed only across those two specific crosses. Cells with insufficient distinct GSS respondents draw with replacement. An education-skew correction (derived from Voelkel et al.'s published achieved-vs-target recruitment gap) is applied within stratum; no comparable correction was applied to income or party (no equivalent published skew evidence for those fields). A fresh, independent persona draw is generated per condition. Known lossy recodes, disclosed: GSS's education variable has no Master's/Doctorate distinction (both collapse to "Master's degree / Professional degree"); GSS's income brackets do not align exactly with the target study's five brackets and were mapped by bracket-midpoint (three boundary-straddling brackets individually documented in the pipeline source).

## E · Stimulus and survey administration
- **E.1 Stimulus presentation**: Verbatim; exact intervention texts as extracted from the target study's own questionnaire materials, no paraphrasing. Control-condition respondents receive one of three filler texts, selected at random per respondent, matching the human study's control design.
- **E.2 Survey walk-through**: The full 43-item survey battery is presented as a single structured-JSON-schema request per persona×condition×repeat (not administered item-by-item or in blocks). The newsletter-signup item is administered as a separate, second conversational turn per the human study's own separate-page design. Item presentation order is randomized independently on every single call: both the order of the battery's thematic blocks (e.g. the trust subscales, the institutional-trust block, the policy-support block) and the order of items within each block are freshly shuffled per call, matching the target study's own preregistered practice of randomizing secondary/tertiary outcome block order for human respondents. The JSON response schema itself is kept in a fixed, canonical key order regardless (real human respondents never saw a JSON schema at all — it is a parsing artifact of this pipeline, not part of "item order" as anyone actually experiences it; only the question text a respondent reads is what varies). No attention-check or comprehension-check handling was implemented.
- **E.3 Response elicitation**: Structured JSON output (enforced via the serving engine's response_format=json_object constraint); each survey item returned as a numeric value on its documented scale. donation elicited as an integer 0–10; newsletter elicited as a "Yes"/"No" string, converted to binary at the derived-variable stage. No token log-probabilities used.

## F · Stochasticity and aggregation
- **F.1 Runs & seeds**: 10 independent generation repeats per persona×condition. No fixed generation-level seed (vLLM's internal sampling RNG); results are reproducible in distribution under identical settings, not bit-for-bit. Persona construction (the GSS cross-quota draw) draws a fresh, high-entropy seed automatically at the start of each run by default, with genuinely independent child seeds derived per condition and per construction step via numpy's SeedSequence.spawn() mechanism (not one shared seed reused everywhere). An explicit seed can optionally be supplied for exact reproduction of a specific prior run's persona set on request.
- **F.2 Aggregation rule**: Arithmetic mean across the 10 repeats per persona×condition, submitted as the raw (unrounded, unthresholded) mean, including for nominally integer-valued items like donation_ams.

## G · Validation & post-processing
- **G.1 Human validation**: N/A; no human review of individual model outputs.
- **G.2 Post-processing**: Each response is validated against the exact expected JSON key set for the battery (43 keys) and, separately, the newsletter turn (1 key). Malformed JSON or responses missing any expected key are excluded from that persona's repeat average and logged with an explicit error record, not silently retained as missing/null. Numeric values are defensively range-clipped to their documented scale. Target-study production run failure rate: 11/99,120 generation attempts failed to parse (0.011%). Effective per-condition N after all exclusions: 551 per intervention, 1,096 control, unaffected by the handful of exclusions, since 10 repeats per persona easily absorbs one occasional drop.
- **G.3 Calibration corrections**: This entry submits raw, uncalibrated model output. This is a deliberate methodological choice, not an oversight: a calibration transform fit against Voelkel et al.'s published human effect sizes was tested during development, but the target study's own raw effect magnitudes were found to already be substantially smaller than Voelkel's (which motivated that correction in the first place), such that applying the Voelkel-derived correction risked over-shrinking already-plausible target-study estimates rather than improving them. See J.1.

## J · Internal selection procedure
- **J.1 Design-space search †**: Substantial, disclosed history: (1) Qwen3-235B-A22B was the original model choice; abandoned after multi-node serving, tokenizer, and memory-fit obstacles prevented any real generation being completed with it. (2) Qwen3.6-27B was selected as a smaller, single-node-deployable, recently-released alternative, deliberately choosing this generation over the newer Qwen3.8 release given the latter's lack of independent validation this close to the submission deadline. (3) Compared against Llama 3.3 70B on the Voelkel et al. pilot testbed across multiple internal validation criteria: directional agreement, Spearman/Pearson correlation against Voelkel's published human effect sizes, control-condition variance ratio (relative to Voelkel's reported human SD), duplicate-response rate, and response anchoring, all computed only against this independent, already-published dataset, never against the target study's own (unavailable) human data. (4) Thinking-mode on vs. off was compared for Qwen3.6-27B specifically via a within/between-persona variance decomposition at two sample scales, plus a full pooled-ATE comparison against Voelkel; thinking was disabled based on comparable-or-better diagnostic performance. (5) After discovering and fixing two pipeline bugs, the full Voelkel validation suite was re-run on both models to check whether earlier findings held up against genuinely diverse (rather than accidentally duplicated) persona sampling. Result, disclosed in full rather than selectively: the duplicate-response/"canned answer" pattern originally found in one condition (and initially suspected to reflect a genuine model-level failure to differentiate by respondent party) collapsed to near-zero across every condition, confirmed independently on both Llama 3.3 70B and Qwen3.6-27B — strong evidence that finding was substantially an artifact of the sampling bug, not a robust property of either model, and it is accordingly not treated as a confirmed limitation of this submission. The variance-ratio and human-correlation effects of the same fix were smaller and noisier across repeated draws, and are not claimed as a resolved improvement, only as evidence the fix changes real generation behavior rather than being cosmetic. All internal validation used Voelkel et al.'s already-published, independent human data exclusively; at no point was any target-study human data consulted or available.
